HGLRC ZEUS 60A ESC — COMMAND RANGE AND SAFE LIMITS
=================================================

ESC IDENTIFICATION USED BY THIS PACKAGE
---------------------------------------

HGLRC Zeus 60A BLHeli_32 3S-6S 4-in-1 ESC

Published specifications for the Zeus family list:

- 3S-6S LiPo input
- 60 A continuous current per motor channel
- 65 A burst current per motor channel for 10 seconds
- BLHeli_32 firmware
- DShot and OneShot protocol support (versions vary)
- no BEC


WHAT "100% PWM" MEANS HERE
---------------------------

This Teensy bench firmware uses conventional servo-style ESC pulses:

    stopped/minimum command: 1000 microseconds
    25% command:             1250 microseconds
    50% command:             1500 microseconds
    100% command:            2000 microseconds

Therefore the dashboard must show PWM: 2000 us when the actual command reaches
100%. The app displays the exact value reported by the Teensy; it does not
estimate the PWM from sound or motor speed.

The code constants are:

    THROTTLE_MIN_US = 1000
    THROTTLE_MAX_US = 2000


IMPORTANT: 2000 us IS A COMMAND MAXIMUM, NOT A GUARANTEE OF SAFE LOAD
------------------------------------------------------------------------

The 5010 motor's 360 KV value only predicts approximate no-load RPM per volt.
It does not specify a safe propeller, current, temperature, or mechanical load.
On a fully charged 6S battery, the approximate no-load speed is:

    360 KV x 25.2 V = 9072 RPM

The safe loaded throttle cannot be selected from KV and ESC rating alone. It
requires the exact motor manufacturer's 6S propeller/current table, or a staged
test measuring current, motor temperature, ESC temperature, vibration, and RPM.

Do not treat the 60 A ESC rating as a target. It is an upper electrical rating,
not the recommended operating current for the motor.


WHY A MOTOR CAN LOOK CAPPED EVEN AT 2000 us
-------------------------------------------

If the dashboard reports 2000 us but RPM does not rise as expected, check:

1. Measure the physical ESC signal pulse or protocol with an oscilloscope or
   logic analyzer.
2. Measure the 6S battery voltage while the motor is running, not only at rest.
3. Check every high-current connector and solder joint for voltage drop/heat.
4. Confirm the Zeus ESC recognizes standard PWM input. The Zeus also supports
   faster digital/OneShot protocols; some configurations may need recalibration
   or a later DShot port.
5. Use an optical tachometer. A 360 KV motor sounds slower than a high-KV FPV
   motor even when operating normally.

Do not calibrate or configure the ESC with propellers installed.
